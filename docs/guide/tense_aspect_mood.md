# 4. Tense, Aspect, and Mood

Until now, this guide has been translating Kokanu sentences into the English present tense.

| Kokanu phrase                   | English meaning   |
|---------------------------------|-------------------|
| mi le makan in kuwosi           | I eat fruit      |
| nin le se pi insu               | a person is in the building |

However, these sentences could actually be translated into any tense or aspect, depending on context.

"mi le makan in kuwosi" could well be "I ate fruit", or "I will eat fruit", or "I have been going to eat fruit".

Kokanu does not mark tense, aspect, or mood in its base word forms.  
Instead, these distinctions are expressed with **modifiers**, which behave like ordinary words.  

Tense, aspect, and mood markers are only used when it's unclear from context what is being said, or if the speaker wants to emphasize certain information.
Normally, the tense, aspect, and mood are all clear from context. Context is very important in Kokanu!

---

### 4.1 Tense


In Kokanu, verbs themselves carry **no tense**.  
By default, the time of an action is inferred from context, or simply left vague.

For explicit marking, Kokanu uses **three tense modifiers**:  

| Modifier | Meaning   |
|----------|-----------|
| **matin** | past      |
| **kenca** | present   |
| **melon** | future    |

**Examples:**

| Kokanu phrase                   | English meaning   |
|---------------------------------|-------------------|
| **le kela kenca men mi**        | I play (now)     |
| **pawo le jatan melon in niku** | the dog will find the meat |
| **tu le sema matin in mi**      | you heard me        |

Additionally, tense can be specified not by modifying the verb, but by stating "in [time period]" using **pi**.

| Kokanu phrase                   | English meaning   |
|---------------------------------|-------------------|
| **le kela men mi pi kenca**        | I play in the present     |
| **pawo le jatan pi melon in niku** | the dog will find the meat in the future |
| **pi matin men tu le sema in mi**  | you heard me in the past       |

::: tip New Words
- **matin** = past  
- **kenca** = present  
- **melon** = future  
- **jatan** = to find (a discovery)
- **niku** = meat/muscle
- **sema** = to hear  
:::

::: warning
All tense, aspect, and mood markers are **ordinary modifiers** in Kokanu.  
They have a special grammatical function, but also behave like other modifiers.
:::

### 4.2 Aspect

Aspect modifiers describe how an event unfolds over time, independent of when it happens.  

| Modifier | Meaning                 |
|----------|-------------------------|
| **tepu** | starting / inceptive    |
| **konje**| ending / terminative    |
| **conpu**| repeating / frequentative |
| **conca**| ongoing / progressive   |
| **lito** | unstarted / prospective |
| **mulu** | completed / perfect     |
| **luka** | interrupted / pausative |

**Examples:**

| Kokanu phrase                   | English meaning                    |
|---------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **mi le antomi tepu**           | I start sleeping / I fall asleep   |
| **mi le antomi konje**          | I stop sleeping / I wake up        |
| **mi le antomi conpu**          | I sleep repeatedly / I often sleep |
| **mi le antomi conca**          | I am sleeping                      |
| **mi le antomi lito**           | I am yet to sleep                  |
| **mi le antomi mulu**           | I am done sleeping / I have slept  |
| **mi le antomi luka**           | I sleep interruptedly / I was sleeping but woke for a moment |

::: tip New Words
- **antomi** = asleep  
- **tepu** = starting  
- **konje** = ending  
- **conpu** = repeating  
- **conca** = ongoing  
- **lito** = unstarted, prospective  
- **mulu** = complete, perfect  
- **luka** = interrupted  
:::

#### Tense vs. Aspect

The difference between **tense** and **aspect** can feel unintuitive.  
Compare the following:  

| Kokanu phrase                  | Meaning                                  |
|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **ja le pen lito matin**       | they were yet to understand              |
| **ja le pen mulu melon**       | they will have understood                |

::: warning
Mixing tense and aspect shows they are not the same.  
For example, **ja le pen matin melon** (“they understood in the future, in the past”) is paradoxical.
:::

::: tip New Words
- **pen** = to understand (a concept)
:::

### 4.3 Mood

Kokanu expresses **mood** through special particles.  
Unlike tense and aspect, mood is less about time and more about the **speaker’s attitude** toward the action.  

#### The particle **o**

The particle **o** is very versatile:  
- **Imperative**: commands (*do X!*)  
- **Suggestive**: proposals (*let’s do X*)  
- **Optative**: wishes (*may X happen*)  

| Kokanu phrase           | Meaning                        |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **o sema ki mi**         | listen to me! (imperative)      |
| **mi o jatan in meja mi**    | let's find my cat / we should find my cat (suggestive) |
| **o tope pi melon**      | may you be fine in the future (optative) |

::: tip New Words
- **tope** = good
:::

#### Other mood markers

- **hon** — context / if → used for **conditionals** (chapter 7.1)  
- **kate** — hypothetical (“as if”, counterfactual)  
- **hakiki** — authentic, realis (“indeed, actually”)  

| Kokanu phrase                               | Meaning                                        |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **hon konco, men mi le pasan**      | In the context of job/ Regarding my job, I am happy                      |
| **hon pilu kate, men mi le pasan** | In the theoretical context of victory, I'd be happy (hypothetical) |
| **mi le pasan hakiki**            | I am really / actually happy (authentic)               |

::: tip New Words
- **o** = imperative / suggestive / optative marker  
- **hon** = context / if (conditional)  
- **kate** = hypothetical (as if)  
- **hakiki** = authentic / realis  
- **konco** = job/profession  
- **tope** = good  
:::

---

### Overview

| Category | How marked in Kokanu |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Tense** | via modifiers: matin (past), kenca (present), melon (future) |
| **Aspect** | via modifiers: tepu, konje, conpu, conca, lito, mulu, luka |
| **Mood** | via particles: **o** (imperative / suggestive / optative), **hon** (conditional), **kate** (hypothetical), **hakiki** (authentic) |
