5. Clauses and Questions

Kokanu provides two main tools for building questions and complex sentences: demonstratives (including ci as a question marker), the yes/no particle ka, and the clause markers ta and te. Together they allow both precise questioning and flexible sentence nesting.

5.1 Demonstratives

Kokanu has three demonstratives: ne, na, and ci.

  • ne → “this”
  • na → “that”
  • ci → interrogative determiner (“which / what / where / why”)

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
ne le losothis is bad
mi le suki in na pawoI like that dog

Unlike modifiers, demonstratives go before the nouns that they modify.

Kokanu phraseMeaning
na ninthat person
ne mejathis cat
ci kuwosiwhat fruit

When demonstratives go after the nouns that they modify, they have a different meaning. This meaning can be roughly translated as "[demonstrative] kind of".

Kokanu phraseMeaning
nin nathat kind of person
meja nethis kind of cat
kuwosi ciwhat kind of fruit

ci as a Question Word

By replacing the item of inquiry with ci, any interrogative can be formed.

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
tu le makan in ci?What do you eat?
ja le makan konje nenka ci?Why do they stop eating?
pawo le antomi pi ci?Where/When does the dog sleep?

As mentioned before, when ci is modifying a content word, the placement of ci can affect the meaning.

When ci is placed before, it's asking for the object, when it's placed after, it's asking for the modifier.

Kokanu phraseMeaning
pawo le antomi pi jan ci?The dog is asleep at a what place?
pawo le antomi pi ci jan?Where is the dog asleep?
pawo le antomi pi ten ci?The dog is asleep at a what time?
pawo le antomi pi ci ten?When is the dog asleep?
canwa ci le makan in makan mi?A what animal ate my food?
ci canwa le makan in makan mi?What animal ate my food?

A possible answer to "pawo le antomi pi ci jan" would be "pi insu" (at a building).

Whereas a possible answer to "pawo le antomi pi jan ci" would be "pi jan tope" (in a good place).

New Words

  • nenka = because (causative preposition)
  • loso = bad
  • jan = place
  • ten = time
  • canwa = animal

5.2 The Particle ka

Yes–no questions are formed with ka. It can appear at the start, end, or both.

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
ka ne makan le tope?Is this food good?
tu le antomi ka?Do you sleep?
ka pata le okutu ka?Is the book hidden?

Remember that pata can both be used as a verb meaning "to read" and a noun meaning "document/thing you read".

New Words

  • okutu = hidden, covered

5.3 The Clause Marker ta

ta introduces subordinate clauses. Kokanu has two types, similar to English:

  • Adverbial clauses (because, when, if…)
  • Relative clauses (that, which…)

Adverbial Clauses

Placed after a preposition, a ta-clause behaves like a main clause.
When a ta-clause directly follows the verb of the sentence as the direct object, you may drop in.

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
mi le suki in na makan nenka ta sapole ja le topeI like that food because its flavor is good
mi le wisan (in) ta tu le makan in makan miI know that you ate my food

The in is only optional if the order is verb, then object. Something like:

  • mi ta tu le makan in makan mi le wisan (order: object, then verb) would mean something entirely different.

New Words

  • sapole = to taste (a flavor)

Closing Clauses

Every subclause must be closed (unless final in the sentence).
Closers are: comma (,) or hu.

hu is essentially just a spoken comma. You can use a comma, hu, or both, it doesn't really matter.

Kokanu phrase (variants)Meaning
po ta tu le sema in kota mi, men mi le kota lenso that you can hear what I say, I speak loud
po ta tu le sema in kota mi hu men mi le kota len[exact same meaning as above]

When pronouncing a sentence out loud, a comma marks a pause.

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
ta nin le teka in kuku, le topethe fact that people see birds is good
mi le kanisa ta na le tope kateI think that that would be good
ka in ta nin le teka in ja, men ja le wisan?do they know that someone sees them?
ukiki in ta kuwosi le se, le sukimonkeys like the fact that fruit exists

New Words

  • len = strong
  • kuku = bird
  • kanisa = to think (a thought)

Relative Clauses

A ta-clause follows the word it describes. Inside, ja refers back to that word. If the clause ends with a sole ja in a preposition, that ja can be dropped.
A relative clause must describe the relation between the clause and the word that it describes. "nin ta mi le suki" is incomplete, because it doesn't explain how "mi le suki" and "nin" are related to each other. The correct way would be "nin ta mi le suki in (ja)".

Examples:

Kokanu phraseMeaning
nin ta in ja men mi le sukia person that I like
nin ta mi le suki ina person that I like (exact same meaning)
pawo ta mi le makan in makan jaa dog, whose food I ate
jan ta mi le pasan pi jaa place at which I’m happy
jan ta mi le pasan pia place I’m happy at

New Words

  • pasan = happy

Nested Clauses

ta-clauses can be nested, collapsing inside → out.

Example:

  • mi le kujo nenka ta pi ta mi le koman, men tu le cuma
    → “I am sad because you leave when I come.”
Kokanu phrase (variants)Meaning
nenka ta tu le cuma hon ta mi le koman,, men mi le kujoI am sad because you leave when I come
nenka ta tu le cuma hon ta mi le koman hu men mi le kujo(same, but hu used)
nenka ta tu le cuma hon ta mi le koman hu, men mi le kujo(same, but hu and comma used)
nenka ta tu le cuma hon ta mi le koman hu hu men mi le kujo(same, but multiple hu used)

Note that hu is often considered to close an unspecified number of clauses. The number of clauses that it closes is dependent on context for many speakers.

New Words

  • kujo = sad
  • koman = arriving
  • cuma = leaving

Indirect Questions

Indirect questions are formed by adding ka into a ta-clause. Just like with regular questions, it can go at the beginning and at the end of the clause.

Warning

If your sentence ends with ka at the end of a ta-clause, some ambiguity can arise. Make sure to close the ta-clause in order to ask a regular question, or put ka at the start of the sentence or clause instead to avoid this.

Kokanu phraseMeaning
tu le solu ta ka mi le makanYou ask whether I eat.
mi le no wisan ta ka mi le suki in na ninI don't know whether I like that person
mi le no wisan ta mi le suki in na nin kaI don't know whether I like that person
mi le no wisan ta mi le suki in na nin hu, kaDo I not know that I like that person?

5.4 The Clause Marker te

te is similar to ta, but with key differences:

  1. A te-clause cannot have a subject.
  2. It implies le.

Relative Clauses

te is equivalent to ta ja le in this case.

Kokanu phraseMeaning
nin ta ja le suki in mia person who likes me
nin te suki in misame meaning

Adverbial Clauses

When used on its own, the best word-for-word translation for te is "to". As in, "te makan" would mean "to eat".

Kokanu phraseMeaning
mi le ju (in) te makanI want to eat
te pata, le topeTo read is good / Reading is good
te makan in kuwosi, le topeTo eat fruit is good / Eating fruits is good

Like with ta, when a te-clause directly follows the verb of the sentence as the direct object, you may drop in.
